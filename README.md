## Mission

To create a continuously learning intelligence layer for the world's enterprises, transforming every interaction into intelligence that compounds over time and empowers people and businesses to think, decide, and perform at an extraordinary level.

---

## The Problem

Enterprise conversations contain valuable information, decisions, context, and signals.

Much of that intelligence is difficult to capture while the interaction is happening and difficult to turn into something useful afterward.

The opportunity is to make conversation itself part of the intelligence system.

---

## Phielia

**Phielia is building the intelligence layer for enterprise conversations, starting with real-time coaching for telecom sales calls.**

Phielia is being built to understand what is happening as each conversation unfolds, provide useful intelligence in the moment, structure conversation context, connect that intelligence to enterprise systems, and learn from interactions over time.

The direction is simple:

**Start with telecom sales.**  
**Build the intelligence layer.**  
**Expand across the enterprise.**

## First Principles

I try to start with the problem rather than the technology.

- **Start with the problem.** Understand the underlying constraint before choosing a solution.
- **Question assumptions.** Existing processes are not automatically optimal.
- **Build from fundamentals.** Understand the underlying system instead of hiding important decisions behind unnecessary abstractions.
- **Create radical utility.** Technology should meaningfully improve someone's ability to accomplish something.
- **Move with urgency.** Build, measure, learn, and iterate.
- **Compound intelligence.** Every interaction should have the potential to make the system more useful than it was before.
- **Take complete ownership.** Own the outcome rather than waiting for someone else to solve the constraint.

## How I Build

I learn by building.

I prefer working systems over theoretical systems, and real feedback over assumptions.

I build quickly, observe what happens, understand why, and iterate.

When something does not work, I want to understand why. When an abstraction becomes a constraint, I want to understand what is underneath it. When a problem matters, I want to get close enough to the underlying system to solve it properly.

## What I'm Building

### Phielia

**Real-time intelligence for enterprise conversations.**

Phielia begins with telecom sales and real-time coaching.

The system is being developed around capabilities including:

## Engineering Philosophy

**The schema matters.**  
Good systems begin with good models of the underlying problem.

**Security is architecture.**  
Security should be designed into the system rather than added after the product exists.

**Abstractions should earn their place.**  
Use abstractions when they make the system better, not simply because they are available.

**Infrastructure should accelerate the product.**  
Technology should increase the speed and quality of execution.

**Real users are the feedback loop.**  
Nothing replaces putting software in front of people and seeing what happens.

**Complexity is a cost.**  
Every dependency, abstraction, and system creates something that must eventually be understood and maintained.
