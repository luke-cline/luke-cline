## About

I'm Luke, a self-taught founder and engineer building **Phielia**.

Phielia is building the intelligence layer for enterprise conversations, starting with real-time coaching for telecom sales calls.

The idea is simple: understand what is happening while a conversation is happening, provide useful intelligence in the moment, and turn every interaction into structured context that becomes more valuable over time.

I build the product myself, from the interface and infrastructure to the data systems and AI pipeline behind it.

Before Phielia, I built and experimented with several products. Each attempt taught me something about engineering, markets, execution, and leadership.

Now I'm focused on one thing: **building Phielia.**

## The Problem

Enterprise conversations contain enormous amounts of information.

Most of that information is difficult to capture, understand, and act on while the conversation is actually happening.

Important context is often lost, delayed, or disconnected from the systems that need it.

Phielia is being built around a simple question:

> **What if enterprise software could understand the conversation while it was happening, instead of only analyzing it afterward?**

## Phielia

### Real-time intelligence for enterprise conversations

Phielia is a real-time coaching layer for live enterprise conversations.

Starting with telecom sales calls, Phielia is designed to understand the interaction as it unfolds, surface actionable intelligence to the person having the conversation, and structure the resulting context for the systems around them.

The long-term vision extends beyond coaching.

Phielia is being built toward an intelligence layer where every interaction contributes context that can make people, systems, and future interactions more effective.

**Starting with:** Telecom sales

**Core:** Real-time conversation intelligence

## Engineering Philosophy

**The schema matters.**

Good systems begin with good models of the underlying problem.

**Security is architecture.**

Security should be designed into the system rather than added after the product exists.

**Abstractions should earn their place.**

Use them when they make the system better, not simply because they are available.

**Infrastructure should accelerate the product.**

Technology exists to increase the speed and quality of execution.

**Real users are the feedback loop.**

Nothing replaces putting software in front of people and seeing what happens.

**Complexity is a cost.**

Every additional dependency, abstraction, and system creates something that must eventually be understood and maintained.
